# content-rules.md

**Dubai Junk Collection — SEO Content Rules**
Companion to `keywords.md` (the primary keyword reference). Read both before writing anything.

These rules govern every blog article and every piece of SEO content published on dubaijunkcollection.com. The goal is content that a real Dubai resident would find genuinely useful — written by someone who has actually cleared out villas and apartments across this city — that also happens to rank. If a rule ever forces a choice between "better for the reader" and "better for Google," choose the reader. Google is catching up to that standard anyway.

---

## 0. The one-line test

Before publishing, ask: **"Would someone who just booked a junk removal job — or is about to — find this genuinely helpful, and does it sound like a person who does this work wrote it?"**

If no, it doesn't ship. Everything below is how we get to yes.

---

## 1. Search intent and keyword usage

- **Every article targets exactly one primary keyword** with one clear intent, pulled from `keywords.md`. That keyword defines the article. Do not merge two primary keywords into one post to "save time" — split them.
- **Match the format to the intent.** Question keywords (Q) get a direct answer near the top. Informational (I) get a genuine guide. Cost/commercial-research keywords get honest numbers and ranges. Never answer a "how do I dispose of X" query with a sales page.
- **Secondary keywords support, they don't compete.** Each article may naturally include 3–8 secondary/long-tail keywords from the *same cluster* in `keywords.md`. Pulling secondaries from a *different* cluster is a cannibalization risk — don't.
- **Commercial head terms belong to service and area pages, not blogs.** Terms like "furniture removal dubai," "villa clearance dubai," or "junk removal dubai marina" are owned by their service/area URLs. A blog may *mention* them and link to them, but must never be built to rank for them. (See Section 3.)
- **Never invent keywords to hit a quota.** If a topic doesn't have a real query behind it in `keywords.md`, it's not an article yet.

---

## 2. Natural keyword placement (no stuffing)

- Use the primary keyword where it reads naturally: the H1, the first ~100 words, at least one H2 where it fits, the meta title, and the URL slug. That's enough for relevance.
- **Write the sentence first, fit the keyword second.** If the keyword makes a sentence clumsy, rephrase the sentence or use a natural variation. Google understands synonyms and context; it does not reward "junk removal Dubai" jammed in nine times.
- **No exact-match repetition for its own sake.** As a rough ceiling, the exact primary keyword should appear only a handful of times in a normal article — and every instance should read like it belongs. Variations ("clearing out," "hauling away," "getting rid of") are preferred over robotic repetition.
- **Never keyword-stuff these places:** alt text, headings, the meta description, the first sentence, or lists. Stuffing any of them is an instant fail.
- **Banned patterns:** keyword-loaded sentences that name multiple communities in a row ("junk removal in Marina, JBR, Palm, Downtown and Business Bay"); repeating the city name in every heading; back-to-back sentences that both open with the keyword.

---

## 3. Avoiding keyword cannibalization

This is the rule most likely to quietly damage the site, so it's strict.

- **One intent, one URL — across the whole site.** No two of our pages should be built to rank for the same query. Blogs target informational/question/comparison/cost intent; service pages own commercial service terms; area pages own geo-modified terms.
- **Before creating any article, run the pre-publish overlap check in Section 16.** No exceptions.
- **A blog never competes with a service page.** If a draft is starting to read like a pitch for "office junk removal dubai," stop — that intent belongs to the service page. Reframe the blog to its actual informational angle (e.g. "How to clear an office in Dubai without disrupting the workday") and link to the service page.
- **A blog never competes with an area page.** Do not write "Junk Removal in Dubai Marina" as a blog — that's the area page's job. A blog *can* cover something area-flavoured that the area page doesn't ("What Marina towers require before a bulky-item pickup"), as long as its target query differs.
- **Two blogs never share a primary keyword or near-duplicate intent.** If a proposed article's core question is already answered by an existing post, either update the existing post or pick a genuinely different angle — don't publish a second one.
- **When overlap is unavoidable, consolidate.** Improve and expand the stronger existing page rather than splitting authority across two.

---

## 4. Unique primary topic and angle for every article

- **Every article needs a specific angle, not just a topic.** "Mattress disposal in Dubai" is a topic. "Why Dubai apartment buildings make mattress disposal harder than it looks — and how to do it in one trip" is an angle. Angles are what make content non-generic.
- **The angle should reflect something we actually know** from doing the work: building access quirks, security-desk paperwork, summer heat and timing, villa handover deadlines, what charities will and won't accept. This is the moat competitors writing from a desk can't copy.
- **No two articles may share the same angle**, even on different topics. If everything sounds like "here's why our service is great," the angle is missing.
- Decide the single takeaway a reader should leave with before writing. If you can't name it in one sentence, the article isn't ready.

---

## 5. Heading structure (H1 / H2 / H3)

- **Exactly one H1 per page.** It contains the primary keyword, reads like a real title, and is not identical to the meta title (they can differ).
- **H2s break the article into genuine sections** that map to how a reader thinks, not to a keyword list. A scannable article answers the reader's likely follow-up questions in order.
- **H3s sit under H2s** for sub-points, steps, or examples. Never skip a level (no H3 without a parent H2).
- Headings should be **descriptive and human** — "What it costs to clear a two-bedroom villa" beats "Villa Clearance Cost Dubai." Front-load meaning, not keywords.
- **Question keywords work well as H2s**, answered immediately underneath in 40–55 words, then expanded. This is how we earn featured snippets — honestly, by actually answering.
- Don't create a heading just to place a keyword. Every heading must introduce real content.

---

## 6. Helpful, original, experience-based content

- **Write from experience.** Use concrete specifics: real scenarios ("a fourth-floor flat with no lift"), real constraints (building management sign-off, two-hour access windows), real outcomes. Specifics signal a human who has done this.
- **Teach the reader to make a good decision**, even if that decision is occasionally "do it yourself" or "donate it instead." Genuinely helpful content that sometimes points away from us builds more trust — and more bookings — than content that only ever says "hire us."
- **Include the things only an insider knows:** what slows a job down, what to prep before the crew arrives, what surprises people about disposal rules, seasonal timing. This is the difference between useful and filler.
- **Original framing, examples, and structure** on every piece. Do not paraphrase a competitor's article. Do not restate the same generic "decluttering benefits" intro used everywhere online.
- **No spun or lightly-reworded content.** If two of our articles could be swapped paragraph-for-paragraph, one shouldn't exist.

---

## 7. E-E-A-T and factual accuracy

- **Experience and expertise must be visible**, not claimed. Show it through specifics (Section 6) rather than adjectives like "expert" and "professional."
- **Verify every fact before publishing**, especially: Dubai Municipality rules, recycling/donation logistics, disposal regulations, fines, and anything about what's legal. Rules change — check current sources, don't rely on memory or an old article.
- **Never invent statistics, prices, dates, laws, or citations.** If a specific number isn't verified, describe it in ranges or qualitative terms ("typically a few hundred dirhams, depending on volume") rather than fabricating precision.
- **Prices and regulations carry a light freshness note or are kept general** so the article doesn't go stale or mislead. When we quote our own pricing model, describe how it works (by volume, fixed upfront) rather than pinning exact figures that change.
- **Be honest about "free."** Competitors lean on "free junk removal." When we cover it, explain accurately how free-vs-paid works in this market and where our fixed-price, insured model fits — without disparaging competitors or making claims we can't stand behind.
- Attribute anything genuinely external (a municipality rule, a charity's acceptance policy) to a real, current source.

---

## 8. Internal linking strategy

- **Every blog links to at least one relevant service page and, where relevant, one area page** — placed where it actually helps the reader, not dumped at the bottom.
- **Link with intent flow in mind:** informational articles should guide a ready reader toward the matching service or area page. A mattress-disposal guide links to the mattress/bulky-item service page.
- **Link between related blogs** (2–4 contextual links) to build topic clusters and keep readers on-site — but only where the linked article genuinely continues the thought.
- **Service and area pages generally don't need to link *out* to blogs** except to a genuinely helpful guide; keep those pages conversion-focused.
- **Don't over-link.** A wall of links dilutes value and looks manipulative. Every link must earn its place by being useful to that specific reader at that point.
- **No orphan articles:** every new post must be linked from at least one existing relevant page or hub, so it isn't stranded.

---

## 9. Anchor-text rules

- **Descriptive, varied, natural anchors.** Describe the destination the way a person would: "our villa and apartment cleanout service," "how mattress disposal works in Dubai," "the communities we cover."
- **Avoid exact-match commercial anchors repeated across the site.** Linking to the furniture page as "furniture removal dubai" every single time reads as manipulation. Rotate natural phrasings.
- **Never use generic anchors** like "click here," "read more," or "this page." The anchor should make sense out of context.
- **Anchor text must match the destination's actual intent.** Don't point a "how to dispose of a fridge" anchor at the homepage; point it at the appliance service page.
- One natural link per destination per article is plenty; don't link the same page five times.

---

## 10. Local relevance for Dubai

- **Ground content in Dubai reality** where it genuinely adds value: community names, building/tower access, security-desk paperwork, villa handover norms, summer heat and job timing, Ramadan clear-outs, the expat move-out cycle.
- **Local detail must be real and specific**, not decorative. Naming a community only to place a keyword is stuffing (Section 2). Naming it because that community's buildings genuinely change the job is useful.
- **Don't force locality onto universal topics.** A general "how to decide what to keep when decluttering" article doesn't need "in Dubai" bolted onto every sentence. Add local flavour only where it changes the answer.
- **Respect local context and tone** — professional, discreet, no culturally tone-deaf humour. The brand voice is calm and dependable, not loud.
- Reflect the real audience: expats, landlords, real-estate agents, offices — and the situations that trigger junk removal for each.

---

## 11. Avoiding duplicate, thin, generic, or spun content

- **No thin content.** If an article can't say something specific and useful beyond what a reader already assumed, don't publish it.
- **No duplicate content**, internal or external. Don't reuse intros, section templates, or paragraphs across articles. Each piece is built fresh.
- **No generic AI-sounding filler.** Ban phrases like "in today's fast-paced world," "when it comes to junk removal," "look no further," "we've got you covered," "hassle-free solutions for all your needs." If a sentence could open any company's blog, cut it.
- **No spun content** — reworded versions of our own or others' articles. If it exists to hit a publishing count rather than to help someone, it fails.
- **Substance over surface.** A shorter article that fully answers the query beats a padded one that circles it.

---

## 12. Avoiding repetition and filler

- **Don't restate the same point in three different ways.** Say it once, clearly, then move on.
- **Cut throat-clearing intros.** Get to the value in the first two sentences. Readers arriving from search have a specific question — respect that.
- **No filler transitions** ("Now that we've covered that, let's move on to…"). Let good structure carry the flow.
- **Every paragraph must add something new** — a fact, a step, an example, a caveat. If it only rephrases the previous paragraph, delete it.
- **Vary sentence and paragraph length.** Uniform, medium-length paragraphs are a tell of machine writing. Real writing has rhythm — short punches, longer explanations.
- Avoid over-listing. Not every article should be bullet points; prose is often more human and more authoritative.

---

## 13. Meta title and meta description

**Meta title**
- ~50–60 characters; primary keyword included naturally, ideally near the front.
- Compelling and specific — written for a human deciding whether to click, not just for the crawler.
- Unique across the entire site. No two pages share a meta title.
- Brand suffix ("— Dubai Junk Collection") only when it fits the length; the message comes first.

**Meta description**
- ~140–160 characters; primary keyword included once, naturally.
- Describe what the reader will get and give a reason to click. It's ad copy, not a summary of the H1.
- **No keyword stuffing, no clickbait, no promises the article doesn't keep.**
- Unique per page.
- Written last, once the article's real value is clear — so it reflects the actual content.

---

## 14. Images and alt text

- **Exactly 2 images per article — never more**: 1 featured (hero) image plus 1 relevant in-body image. Full spec in `image-standards.md`.
- **Sourcing priority — generate first, stock second**:
  - **Priority 1**: if image generation is available, generate a unique, article-specific image yourself for each of the two slots.
  - **Priority 2**: if generation is unavailable or can't produce a suitable, relevant image, fall back to the existing Pexels / image sourcing workflow.
- **Both images must earn their place** — illustrate a step, show a before/after, clarify a scenario. No stock filler for decoration's sake.
- **Alt text describes the image for a real person** who can't see it. Accurate, specific, natural.
- **Alt text is not a keyword slot.** Include the keyword only if it honestly describes the image; never stuff it. "Crew wrapping a mattress before carrying it down a Marina stairwell" beats "junk removal dubai mattress removal dubai."
- **Prefer original or genuinely representative images.** Avoid obviously generic stock that undercuts the "we actually do this" positioning.
- Compress images and use descriptive file names (hyphenated, human-readable) for page speed and clarity.
- Every meaningful image needs alt text; purely decorative images can use empty alt.

---

## 15. FAQ usage

- **Add an FAQ only when readers genuinely have follow-up questions** the article didn't already cover in the body. FAQs are not a place to re-stuff keywords or pad length.
- **Each FAQ question must be a real question** (ideally a Q-intent keyword from `keywords.md` that fits this article's cluster) with a genuine, concise answer — 40–70 words.
- **No overlap with the article body.** If the body already answers it, it doesn't belong in the FAQ.
- **Don't duplicate FAQs across articles.** The same question answered on five posts is cannibalization and looks automated.
- **3–6 FAQs max** in most cases. Quality over quantity. If you can only think of one real question, the article doesn't need an FAQ block.

---

## 16. Pre-publish overlap check (run BEFORE writing each article)

This is the gate that prevents cannibalization and duplication. **Complete it before drafting, not after.**

1. **Confirm the primary keyword** in `keywords.md` and note its assigned intent and page type. If it's not marked for a blog, stop — it belongs to a service or area page.
2. **Search existing content** (blog, service pages, area pages) for the primary keyword and its close variations. Check titles, H1s, and target keywords.
3. **Check for intent overlap, not just word overlap.** Ask: is any existing page already built to answer this exact question or serve this exact intent? Word-different but intent-identical still counts as a conflict.
4. **Decide the outcome:**
   - *No overlap* → proceed to draft.
   - *Overlaps an existing blog* → update/expand that post instead, or choose a demonstrably different angle and primary keyword.
   - *Overlaps a service/area page* → do not compete; reframe the blog to a distinct informational angle and link to that page.
5. **Confirm a unique angle** (Section 4) that no existing article uses.
6. **Map the internal links** you'll include (which service page, area page, related blogs) before writing, so linking is intentional, not an afterthought.
7. **Log the article** (primary keyword, intent, angle, target URL, key internal links) in the content tracker so the next writer can run this same check against it.

If steps 1–5 can't all be satisfied, the article isn't ready to write.

## 17. Length based on intent (not arbitrary counts)

- **Length is dictated by the query, not a word target.** Write exactly as much as it takes to fully answer the intent — then stop.
- **Quick question / single-item queries** ("how to dispose of a mattress in Dubai"): often a focused, complete answer. Don't pad it to 1,500 words to look substantial — padding buries the answer and reads as filler.
- **Guides, comparisons, cost breakdowns, move-out/handover topics:** naturally longer because they genuinely have more to cover. Length here should come from real substance, not repetition.
- **The test is completeness, not word count:** has every reasonable follow-up question been answered, and is there nothing left that only repeats what's above? If yes, it's the right length.
- **Never hit a word count by repeating points, adding filler intros, or over-explaining.** A tight 600-word answer that nails the intent beats a bloated 2,000-word one.

---

## 18. CTA usage (helpful, not pushy)

- **Lead with help, close with an offer.** The body earns trust by being useful; the CTA is the natural next step for a reader who's now ready.
- **One primary CTA per article**, placed where a reader would realistically be ready to act (usually after the main answer and again at the end). Booking runs through WhatsApp/phone — make the next step obvious and low-friction.
- **The CTA must match the article's intent.** A cost-guide CTA can offer a fixed WhatsApp quote; a "how to donate furniture" article shouldn't hard-sell paid removal — it can simply mention we handle the items donation won't take.
- **Not every paragraph is a sales pitch.** Articles that pitch in every section read as promotional and lose trust and rankings. If more than a small fraction of the article is selling, rebalance toward help.
- **No fake urgency or pressure.** Calm and dependable is the brand. "Message us a photo and we'll send a fixed price" is on-brand; "Book NOW before slots run out!" is not.

---

## 19. Final editorial QA checklist

Run every article against this before it goes live. Any "no" blocks publication.

**Intent & keywords**
- [ ] One clear primary keyword from `keywords.md`, correct intent, marked for blog use.
- [ ] Secondary keywords are from the same cluster and read naturally.
- [ ] No keyword stuffing anywhere (body, headings, meta, alt, FAQ).

**Cannibalization**
- [ ] Pre-publish overlap check (Section 16) completed and logged.
- [ ] Does not compete with any service page, area page, or existing blog.
- [ ] Unique angle no other article uses.

**Content quality**
- [ ] Genuinely useful; passes the one-line test (Section 0).
- [ ] Reads as human-written — natural rhythm, real specifics, no AI filler phrases.
- [ ] Experience-based detail a desk-writer couldn't fake.
- [ ] No thin, duplicate, spun, generic, or repetitive content.
- [ ] Length fits the intent; nothing padded, nothing missing.

**E-E-A-T & accuracy**
- [ ] Every fact, price, rule, and figure verified or safely generalized — nothing invented.
- [ ] Dubai-specific claims are current and correct.

**Structure & on-page**
- [ ] One H1 (with keyword, human-readable); logical H2/H3 hierarchy, no skipped levels.
- [ ] Meta title unique, ~50–60 chars, natural keyword.
- [ ] Meta description unique, ~140–160 chars, natural keyword, real reason to click.
- [ ] URL slug clean and keyword-appropriate.

**Links & media**
- [ ] Links to ≥1 relevant service page (and area page where relevant), placed naturally.
- [ ] 2–4 contextual internal links to related content where genuinely helpful.
- [ ] Anchor text descriptive and varied; no generic or repeated exact-match anchors.
- [ ] Not an orphan — linked from at least one existing relevant page.
- [ ] Exactly 2 images (1 featured + 1 in-body), generated where possible and sourced only as fallback; alt text accurate and natural, not stuffed.

**Local, FAQ & CTA**
- [ ] Local relevance real and value-adding where used, not decorative.
- [ ] FAQ only if it adds genuine, non-duplicated value (3–6 real Qs).
- [ ] One intent-matched CTA, helpful not pushy; article isn't overly promotional.

**Final read**
- [ ] Read aloud once. If any sentence sounds robotic, repetitive, or salesy, fix it before publishing.
